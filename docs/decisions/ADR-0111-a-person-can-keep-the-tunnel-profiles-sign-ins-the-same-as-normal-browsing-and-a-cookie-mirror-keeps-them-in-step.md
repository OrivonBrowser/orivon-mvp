# ADR-0111: A person can keep the tunnel profiles' sign-ins the same as normal browsing, and a cookie mirror keeps them in step

- **Status:** accepted. Amends [`ADR-0084`](ADR-0084-the-tunnel-can-carry-the-whole-browser-and-a-website-with-an-entry-lives-in-one-of-two-shared-profiles.md) (where a website's sign-ins live while the new setting is off) and carries out `d-0784`. *Provisional* on the scope rule (which cookies are shared), settled by the owner's word after first use; see Reversibility.
- **Date:** 2026-11-09; the scope narrowed 2026-11-10 after a review found that the first version shared the whole cookie jar, and the rule for adding a site narrowed the same day after a second review found that a redirect hop could add a tracker.
- **Type:** product / security
- **Decided by:** owner

## Decision

**A setting, on by default: "Keep tunneled sites separate"** (`network.tunnel.separateSignIns`, Settings under Tunnels). On, everything in [`ADR-0084`](ADR-0084-the-tunnel-can-carry-the-whole-browser-and-a-website-with-an-entry-lives-in-one-of-two-shared-profiles.md) stands: the profile sessions (`persist:tunnel-profile`, `persist:proxy-profile`, `persist:direct-profile`) hold cookies of their own, apart from the default session's.

**Off, the cookies of the sites a person opens in a tunnelled tab are kept the same in that profile and in the default session, in both directions.** A tunnelled tab keeps its route and its session exactly as before (every request still leaves by the tab's tunnel, a website's third-party requests included) and sends the cookies normal browsing holds for the sites it shows; a sign-in made in either place is in the other. What a site keeps in page storage (localStorage, IndexedDB, caches, service workers) stays apart. The copy is `src/main/route/cookie-mirror/`:

- **The scope: a site is mirrored into a profile when the person starts a navigation to it, or when a navigation to it has committed as the top-level page of a tab in that profile.** A site is its registrable domain by the whole public suffix list, its private section included (`mirror-site.ts`): `www.shop.example` and `cdn.shop.example` are one site, `alice.github.io` and `bob.github.io` are two, and every cookie whose domain is the site or below it is part of it. An address Orivon serves (`<content>.ipfs.orivon`, a Swarm host, a `.eth` name) is its whole host, since the domain above it is a namespace shared by every party in it. The cookies of any other domain are never mirrored: a tracker a page embeds, a third-party widget, the partitioned cookie an embed sets under the site. A tracker whose ID is minted during a tunnelled visit therefore never reaches normal browsing, and one minted in normal browsing never reaches the tunnel profile. The scope is kept for each profile (a site opened in the proxy profile is not mirrored into Nym's), and is *provisional*.
- **Before the page loads, and after it.** A main-frame request in a profile session for a site that is being copied into the profile waits for the copy, at most 8 seconds, whatever started the load. A site new to the profile is added before the page asks only for the first request of a navigation the person started (the address bar, a bookmark, history, a restored tab): such a request carries no initiating origin and no referrer, and is not a hop after the first of its navigation (a redirect keeps the request's id). The site is then recorded, and the default session's cookies of it are written into the profile, before the request goes on. A redirect hop never adds a site, and nor does a navigation a page started (a link, a script's `location`, a form, a popup opened with a referrer): a tracker a tunnelled page bounces through (`tracker.example/r?u=site`) is not in the scope, because it is never the page a tab commits at. A page-started navigation to a site that is new to the profile commits signed out once; when it has committed, the site is added and its cookies are copied in as for any other (what the signed-out load set in the profile is replaced by the default session's), and a reload is signed in. A profile session made after the switch was moved, or a restart, first makes the profile equal to the default session for the sites recorded for it (the same wait on the profile's first request).
- **Equal means the default session wins.** At that point, and at every start, the profile gets what the default session holds of the site, and loses what only it holds of the site: a cookie is never copied from a profile to the default session at launch or when a site is first copied. The cost: a sign-in made in a tunnelled tab just before a crash, or the profile's own cookies of a site from before the setting was moved, are replaced by the default session's. Only live changes go both ways.
- **The record is kept on disk** (`cookie-mirror.json` in the profile folder): the sites copied into each profile, written before the first cookie of a site is, so a restart keeps the scope and a clear can find everything that was copied. A site that cannot be recorded is not mirrored. It is a plaintext list of the sites opened in tunnelled tabs; it is emptied when the setting is turned back on and when history is cleared.
- **Following.** Each session's `cookies` `changed` event is followed for cookies of the sites recorded. A change is not copied from the event: the cookie is read again in both sessions by address and name, and compared with the value the two last agreed on. The side that changed since gives to the other. When both did, the change observed later wins (the order events reach the mirror in, not a cookie's creation time). A cookie changed again before it is carried is carried once. As a bound against a store that keeps changing what it is given, a cookie is written into one session at most 30 times in 5 seconds.
- **Which changes count.** An insert, a change of attributes, a removal by a person or a site (`explicit`), and a site's deletion by sending the cookie already expired (`expired-overwrite`, which is how a sign-out arrives). Not the removal half of an overwrite (an insert follows), not a cookie that ran out (`expired`) and not one evicted from a full store: each session lets those go by itself.
- **A removal reaches the other session only while that session still holds the value last agreed on**; if it changed since, the later change wins and is carried back. A removal the mirror made itself is never carried: a removal by address and name takes every cookie of that name the address would send, the mirror writes the others back, and one it cannot write back is forgotten, so the next look copies it from the other session and never removes it there.
- **Attributes.** A host-only cookie is written with no domain, so it stays host-only; a domain cookie keeps its domain; `secure`, `httpOnly`, `sameSite`, path and expiry are kept, and a session cookie is written with no expiry, so it stays one.
- **Partitioned cookies** cannot be told apart. Electron 44's `Cookie` and `CookiesSetDetails` carry no partition key, and `cookies.get` lists a partitioned cookie like any other (measured: one set by a page under its own site and one set by an embedded frame both come back, with no mark). A cookie of a site that is mirrored and shares a name, domain and path with another in a listing is never written to the other session, and is removed from it only when the cookie last mirrored is removed; a cookie that is set anew is judged afresh, so a look-alike that has gone since does not keep a new sign-in from being carried. **Known limit:** a lone partitioned cookie of a mirrored site is written unpartitioned, as an ordinary `SameSite=None` cookie (a listing, `equalize` or a change, shows it with no mark), and is then sent by every request to that site from the other session, whichever top-level site makes it. Third-party cookie blocking does not cover that: it works on the request, and a script inside a cross-site frame can still read and write `document.cookie` (`src/main/privacy/README.md`). The exposure is limited to the cookies of a site the person opened and an embed of the same site set; the scope rule is what bounds it.
- **Which sessions.** Only the kept (`persist:`) session of each of the three profiles and the default session. Never a private window's process (it runs no mirror), a profile kept in memory (plain-http origins), an app's session (`persist:app-*`), a temporary session or an internal one.
- **Turning it back on** stops following and clears, from each profile, the cookies of the sites recorded for it; the profile's cookies of any other site stay, and so does everything in the default session. A clear owed is written to the record before anything is cleared and removed from it only after every profile has been cleared, so a failure, a crash or a restart before the clear finishes leaves it owed: the next start, with the setting on, clears first. Settings says in one line that tunnelled sites ask for sign-in once.
- **Clear browsing data** forgets the sites listed when any history is cleared (an hour, a day, a week or all): the list carries no times, so it cannot be cut by range. The profiles' copies of those sites' cookies are cleared as when the setting is turned back on (a clear owed is recorded first), the list on disk is emptied, and the mirror goes on; a site is added again as it is opened. The default session's cookies are the business of the site-data choice of that dialog, as before. A tunnelled tab is then signed out of those sites until it opens them again.
- **`orivon://report`** states the mirror's state, how many profile sessions it keeps in step (none while apart) and how many cookies it wrote, removed and failed to write (`diagnostics.browser.signIns`): counts and one word, never a cookie's name, value or site.

## Context

[`ADR-0084`](ADR-0084-the-tunnel-can-carry-the-whole-browser-and-a-website-with-an-entry-lives-in-one-of-two-shared-profiles.md) keeps a tunnelled website in a profile session of its own so that its sign-ins cannot link the tunnelled visits to the person's accounts. The owner, 2026-11-09: tunnels run on a separate session to preserve identity, "but users should be allowed to disable this behavior in settings, to keep the same accounts even while tunnels are enabled". Shown two ways, the owner chose to share sign-ins and keep each tab's own route.

## Alternatives considered

- **Mirror the whole cookie jar** (the first version). Rejected after review: a tracker's ID minted during a Nym visit would be sent from normal browsing on every site, which goes well past "the same accounts". The sites a person opens are the accounts.
- **Mirror the cookies a page of the site sends, by request.** Not chosen: it needs a hook on every request and misses a sign-in that lives on a domain the page only embeds; it stays a candidate if the scope rule is narrowed (Reversibility).
- **Add a site at every main-frame request, redirect hops included** (the second version). Rejected after review: a site that bounces a tab through a tracker's redirect (`tracker.example/r?u=site`), or a script's `location`, or a popup, adds the tracker to the scope for good, its normal-browsing cookies are copied in, and every tunnelled site that embeds the tracker then sends the normal identity.
- **Add a site only when its navigation commits, never before the first request.** Not chosen: the page the person typed would load signed out and be signed in only on a reload, which is the thing the setting is for. The address bar, a bookmark, history and a restored tab are the person's own choice of site, so their first request may add one.
- **Treat a navigation a page started as the person's choice, to copy before its first request.** Not chosen: the page could then make a tab visit any host first, with the normal identity attached, and learn it. A page can always make its tab commit at another site, and the site is then added; what the rule keeps out is the visit that never shows as a page.
- **Route by site name in the default session** (a website that has an `on` entry stays in the default session and the session's proxy rules send its host through the tunnel). Rejected by the owner: a proxy chosen by host name in one session leaves a site's third-party requests outside the tunnel, and it cannot give one tab a tunnel its neighbour lacks.
- **Add the default session's cookies to each profile request in `webRequest`.** Rejected: `Set-Cookie` and `document.cookie` writes of the tunnelled page would still need carrying back, httpOnly cookies and every redirect would have to be handled by hand, and it is work on every request instead of on every change.
- **Remember what the mirror wrote and ignore the matching events.** Rejected: a write that changes nothing may raise no event (or a different one), and a remembered expectation that is never met swallows a real change later. Reading both sessions' current state, and comparing it with the value the two last agreed on, needs no expectation of an event and ends when they agree.
- **Follow `expired` and `evicted` removals too.** Rejected: a cookie that ran out is gone from every session at its expiry, and one evicted because a single store was full would sign a site out of the others.
- **Leave the profiles' cookies where they are when the setting is turned back on.** Rejected by the owner's wording ("so the tunneled identity is separate again"): the copied sign-ins would stay linked to normal browsing.
- **Clear every cookie of a profile when the setting is turned back on.** Not chosen: the profile also holds what a tunnelled site was given while the setting was on (its own identity), which the mirror never touched, and the clear is meant to undo only what the mirror did.
- **Let a late profile join, or a site load, without waiting.** Rejected: the first page of a profile made while the setting is off would load signed out and sign in on a reload.

## Reasoning

A copy driven by the session's own change events is the smallest thing that keeps the tunnel's route and storage as they are and still shares the one thing the owner named, sign-ins. Limiting it to the sites a person opens keeps the tunnelled identity of everything else (the trackers a page embeds) apart. Reading the current state rather than trusting an event keeps it from looping and from carrying a stale value; remembering what both sides last agreed on is what tells a removal from a change, a conflict from a one-sided edit. The default session being the truth at launch is what stops a profile's stale or lone cookie from coming back into normal browsing. A site is added where the person chose it or where a tab has landed, never where a request merely passed, because that is the difference between the sites a person opens and the hosts a page sends them through.

## Consequences

- While the setting is off, a website the person opens in a tunnelled tab sees the accounts of normal browsing: that link between a tunnelled visit and the person's accounts is what the person chose, for that site. The tunnel still hides the computer's address from the site, and a third party the page embeds still sees a tunnel identity of its own.
- A sign-in that lives on a domain the page only embeds or calls (a separate login or API domain that is never the top-level page of a tab) is not shared and asks again. A login that navigates the tab to that domain is a top-level page and is shared.
- A page-started navigation to a new site loads signed out once, and the site is added when it has committed (a reload is signed in): a link followed in a tunnelled tab to a site not opened there before asks for sign-in once, and what that first load set in the profile is replaced by the default session's cookies of the site.
- A navigation counts as the person's when its first request has neither an initiating origin nor a referrer. A tab a page opens with no referrer at all (`rel=noreferrer`, or a page whose policy sends none) cannot be told from one the person opened, so its site is added at the first request; the cost is a visible new tab, and the site would be added when the tab commits anyway.
- Clearing history signs the tunnelled tabs out of every site that was listed, and forgets which sites were opened in tunnels; the sites are listed again as they are opened.
- A site is its registrable domain by the whole public suffix list, so the customers of a service that hands out domains (`github.io`, `cloudfront.net`, `eth.limo`) are each a site, and an address Orivon serves is its whole host: every content address, Swarm hash and `.eth` name (and every subname of a registrar's, such as `alice.base.eth`) is a party of its own. The repository's `siteOf` for privacy keeps the private section out on purpose (it reads `github.io` as one site); the mirror does not use it.
- Only cookies are shared. A site that keeps its session in localStorage or IndexedDB signs in again in a tunnel profile.
- A change in either place reaches the other: a person who clears cookies of a site in normal browsing clears them in the profiles that mirror it, and removing a website's storage from the tunnel list in Settings also removes its cookies from normal browsing while the setting is off.
- At every start the profile is made equal to the default session for its sites: a sign-in made in a tunnelled tab just before a crash is lost, and a site's cookies in a profile from before the setting was moved are replaced the first time a tunnelled tab opens it.
- A sign-out removes a cookie of the other session that is listed with a partitioned look-alike, and with it the look-alike, which cannot be written back.
- The notice before a website first moves into a profile says the person stays signed in, and that only its other storage stays apart, while the setting is off (`SiteTunnel.signInsShared`).
- Turning the setting back on deletes the profiles' copies of the sites for good; the extensions' own storage in the profiles is not touched.
- Work: one change event per cookie change in the four cookie stores, filtered by site at once, and a lookup scoped by address in each of two sessions for a change that is carried; a site's first copy is two reads of the jar (Electron's `domain` filter is applied after the jar is read) and one write for each difference. A page load of a new site in a profile waits for that copy, up to 8 seconds.
- One worker does the copies, the clears and the carrying in turn, and a profile's join or a site's first copy goes ahead of the carrying of changes.

## Reversibility

- **Cost to reverse:** cheap. The mirror is additive and the setting's default is the earlier behaviour; the one thing that does not come back is a profile's copies of the mirrored sites once the setting has cleared them.
- **What would make us revisit:** an Electron cookie API that carries the partition key (to carry partitioned cookies as they are, and to leave out every one now carried as an ordinary cookie), or a way to run a tunnelled website with its own route inside the default session without letting its third-party requests out (to replace the copy with one jar).
- **What settles the scope rule** (sites opened as the top-level page of a tunnelled tab by a person's navigation or a committed one, by registrable domain with the private section of the public suffix list): the owner's word after first use, on whether a sign-in on a domain a site only embeds must be shared as well (widen the rule), on whether a page-started navigation should also be copied before its first request (the cost is the visit a page can force), and on whether clearing history should keep the list for the sites it is not asked to forget (it cannot be cut by range now).
