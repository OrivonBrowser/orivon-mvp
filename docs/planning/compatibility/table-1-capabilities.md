# Table 1: the capability surface (authority)

One part of the [compatibility matrix](../compatibility-matrix.md). The matrix page has this
table in readable form, one row per topic; this page lists every item one by one, for looking up a
single name. It says what works today and nothing else.

## Table 1a: what orivon.* offers

This table names every member of [`capability-api.ts`](../../../src/contracts/capability-api.ts) and [`handles.ts`](../../../src/contracts/handles.ts) (7 root members, 26 methods, 9 handle interfaces with 21 handle methods, 2 events), all 15 `CapabilityKind` values and 12 error codes of [`manifest.ts`](../../../src/contracts/manifest.ts) and [`errors.ts`](../../../src/contracts/errors.ts), all 19 `LIMITS` keys, and every manifest field that changes what an app may do.

|---|:--:|:--:|:--:|:--:|---|
| `orivon.version`, `app.manifest()`, `app.grants()` | ✅ | ✅ | ✅ | ➖ | `version` is the frozen number `0`. `manifest()` returns the pinned manifest and backs Electron's `app.getVersion`. `grants()` returns what is granted, a subset of what the manifest declares, as `Grant` records (`id`, `origin`, `capability`, `patterns`, `grantedAt`). e2e: `grants()` in [`e2e-capability-boundary.test.ts`](../../../test/capabilities/e2e-capability-boundary.test.ts) and [`e2e-site-info.test.ts`](../../../test/e2e-site-info.test.ts), `manifest()` in [`e2e-extensions-orivon-filter.test.ts`](../../../test/e2e-extensions-orivon-filter.test.ts) |
| Where `window.orivon` exists | ✅ | ✅ | ✅ | ➖ | A frozen, non-writable object in the top document of every ordinary tab. An iframe of the app and a `web.context` or `<webview>` document have none; a same-origin popup has one; a child started by the shim gets a relayed copy. A dedicated `Worker` the app writes has none (not measured). If the preload cannot install the object in the main world, every method refuses and `net` keeps only `lookup` |
| Who may call `window.orivon` | ✅ | ✅ | ✅ | ➖ | A frame with no authenticated origin, or in the wrong session, fails `denied`. From an origin with no registered manifest, `manifest()` fails `internal` and every capability method fails `denied`. Every method refuses a caller whose stack shows a `chrome-extension:` frame or no page frame ([ADR-0045](../../decisions/ADR-0045-window-orivon-refuses-extension-code-a-filter-not-a-sandbox.md)) |
| `app.requestGrant` | ✅ | ✅ | ✅ | ➖ | A page's call becomes a consent dialog and resolves `true` or `false`; an accepted answer persists a grant later calls use. The decision runs in the main process's request-grant subsystem over the broker's ledger, and `createBroker` returns no `requestGrant` of its own. It resolves `false` for an undeclared kind, a widening pattern set, a capability the person declined, `web.context` and `web.embed`. `CapabilityRequest.patterns` absent means what the manifest declares. Page budget 120 s. A first visit can see an early call denied before the dialog resolves (accepted). No real-Electron e2e calls it from a page; proof is unit tests in [`ipc-request-grant.test.ts`](../../../src/broker/transport/tests/ipc-request-grant.test.ts) |
| `net.connect` (TCP out) | ✅ | ✅ | ✅ | ⚠️ | A `TcpSocket` with both stream halves and the resolved remote address, matched against `tcp.connect` patterns after resolution; e2e-verified. Node shape in [`net/socket.ts`](../../../src/shim/net/socket.ts): `connect()` reads `host` and `port` only: `localAddress`, `localPort`, `family`, `lookup`, `hints` and `autoSelectFamily` are ignored, `path` refuses by name, and the dial races the resolved addresses under one 30 s deadline |
| `net.connect` pattern grammar (`tcp.connect`, `udp.send`, `https.connect`) | ✅ | ✅ | ✅ | ➖ | `host:port`, at most 256 per grant. Host: `*` (public unicast only), paired for `tcp.connect` and `https.connect` with `*`, a port or a port range (`*:6697`, `*:8000-8999`) and for `udp.send` only as `*:*`, an exact hostname, a canonical IPv4 or bracketed IPv6 literal, or `localhost` (both loopbacks, never resolved); a decimal or octal spelling of an address (`2130706433`, `0177.0.0.1`) authorises nothing. Port: `N`, `N-M`, or `*` beside a named host; port 0 and leading zeros are rejected. A `udp.send` pattern with a `*` host and any other port (`*:53`) is rejected at manifest load and by `app.requestGrant`; no sub-glob exists; a CIDR string, a `.local` name and a `.onion` name are accepted and match nothing. For `tcp.connect` and `udp.send`, a private, loopback, link-local or CGNAT address is reachable only through a literal written in the pattern. `https.connect` matches the name, not the answer; its `*` excludes `localhost` and non-public literals |
| Destinations a grant does not reach by default | ✅ | ✅ | ✅ | ➖ | Ports 23, 25, 53, 139, 445, 465, 587, 3389, 6667 and 6697 are reachable (by all three outbound kinds) only through a pattern that spells that exact port, `*:6697` included for `tcp.connect` and `https.connect`. For `tcp.connect` and `udp.send`, a hostname never authorises a private answer, and multicast and `255.255.255.255` are refused even to a literal, while a directed broadcast such as `192.168.1.255` passes the check and fails at the socket (Table 1b). Any outbound call while a system proxy applies to the destination is refused. Every denial is the uniform `denied`, with no `platformCode` |
| `net.connectSecure` (TLS out) | ✅ | ✅ | ✅ | ⚠️ | TLS ends on the trusted side ([ADR-0017](../../decisions/ADR-0017-orivon-owns-the-app-http-path.md)), matched by hostname against `https.connect`. `SecureConnectOptions` carries `rejectUnauthorized`, `ca` (which replaces the built-in roots), `cert`, `key`, `pfx`, `passphrase`, `servername` and `alpnProtocols`, bounded (64 KiB per `cert` or `key`, 64 CA entries, 512 KiB of CA text); an option that unbinds the name adds a resolve-once address check. The Node shim drops `minVersion`, `maxVersion`, `ciphers`, `ecdhCurve`, `session` and `crl` without an error, and `tls.connect({ socket })` (STARTTLS) refuses by name (A226) |
| `SecureTcpSocket` handshake report | ✅ | ✅ | ✅ | ⚠️ | `authorized`, `authorizationError`, `alpnProtocol` and `peerCertificate` (`subject`, `issuer`, `subjectaltname`, `valid_from`, `valid_to`, `serialNumber`, `fingerprint`, `fingerprint256`, `raw`, `pubkey`), captured once at the handshake. No protocol version, cipher, session or chain: the shim's `getProtocol()` is `null` and `getCipher` is absent |
| `net.listen` (TCP in) | ✅ | ✅ | ✅ | ⚠️ | IPv4 only, ports 1024 and up inside a declared range (`*` rejected; port 0 picks inside the range). `scope` picks the interface and the grant (next row). Refused `denied` while a system proxy is configured. The handle is tied to the grant that authorised it, and `localAddress` reports the address bound. Each accepted socket is a real handle delivered over the server's own port. The shim's [`net/server.ts`](../../../src/shim/net/server.ts) refuses `listen(path)`. Real-Electron e2e: a page listens, serves and closes in [`e2e-http-server.test.ts`](../../../test/node-runtime/e2e-http-server.test.ts) and [`e2e-embed-local.test.ts`](../../../test/capabilities/e2e-embed-local.test.ts); a spawned component listens in [`e2e-child-process.test.ts`](../../../test/node-runtime/e2e-child-process.test.ts) and [`e2e-child-host.test.ts`](../../../test/node-runtime/e2e-child-host.test.ts) |
| `scope` on `listen` and `udpBind`; `tcp.listen.local` and `udp.bind.local` grants | ✅ | ✅ | ✅ | ➖ | ([ADR-0034](../../decisions/ADR-0034-listening-is-local-unless-the-app-declares-the-network.md)) `'local'`, the default when `scope` is omitted, binds `127.0.0.1`, even under a `.network` grant, and is authorised by the `.local` grant or the `.network` one, the narrower tried first. `'network'` binds `0.0.0.0` and needs the `.network` grant: a `.local` grant never stands in for it (`denied`). Any other value is `invalid`. An app that holds only a `local` kind can bind loopback inside its ports and nothing wider. The page forwards `scope` (omitted stays omitted) and the control channel validates it |
| `listen` and `bind` host in the Node shim (`net.Server#listen`, `dgram.Socket#bind`) | ➖ | ➖ | ➖ | ⚠️ | `127.0.0.1`, `::1` and `localhost` ask for `'local'`; no host, `0.0.0.0` and `::` ask for `'network'`, asked again as `'local'` when that is `denied`, so `listen(3000)` works under a `.local` grant and `address()` reads `127.0.0.1`. Any other single address refuses by name, for TCP and for UDP. `::1` and `::` bind the IPv4 addresses (A218). A `wasm32-wasip2` component's bind maps the same way, except that a single non-loopback address binds every interface |
| `net.udpBind` + `UdpSocket` | ✅ | ✅ | ✅ | ⚠️ | IPv4 (`udp4`) only, under `udp.bind.local` or `udp.bind.network` by `scope`, as `net.listen`'s (previous rows); a `'local'` bind is `127.0.0.1` and a `'network'` one `0.0.0.0`. Each send is checked against `udp.send` patterns, so outbound UDP needs a bound socket. One chunk is one datagram (at most 65507 bytes). A refused send never rejects: it counts in `droppedOutbound` and `refusals`. e2e in [`e2e-udp-capability.test.ts`](../../../test/capabilities/e2e-udp-capability.test.ts). The `dgram` shim reads the port and the `bind` address (`bind(port, address)` or `bind({ port, address })`) and drops `type`, `reuseAddr` and buffer sizes; it reports a refused send as success and omits 15 `Socket` members ([Table 3d](table-3d-network.md)) |
| Bind pattern grammar (`tcp.listen.*`, `udp.bind.*`) | ✅ | ✅ | ✅ | ➖ | Bare port ranges (`22000`, `6881-6889`) per scope. `*` is rejected, a range must start at 1024 or above (privileged ports are denied at every tier), and port 0 picks a random port inside the ranges, never an OS-chosen one |
| `net.lookup` (`orivon.net.lookup`) | ✅ | ✅ | ✅ | ➖ | No separate `orivon.dns` namespace exists. Answers A and AAAA in resolver order, public unicast only: an answer that is not public is dropped, and a name left with none rejects `unreachable`. The host must be named by a held `tcp.connect` or `udp.send` pattern (or `*`); `https.connect` and an address-literal pattern do not authorise a lookup. A pattern that names `localhost` gets both loopbacks from a constant; under `*` its loopback answers are dropped |
| `dns.lookup` over `net.lookup` | ➖ | ➖ | ➖ | ⚠️ | [`net/dns.ts`](../../../src/shim/net/dns.ts) offers `lookup` only, with `hints`, `verbatim` and `order` accepted and ignored. The person revokes it in the permissions panel or the site-info popover, and revoking closes every handle it authorised (RST, `revoked`); revoking `web.embed` clears the embed script, and picked paths outlive an `fs` revoke. Handles also die when the origin's last document closes |
| Per-origin allowances (`LIMITS`, part 1) | ✅ | ✅ | ➖ | ➖ | `concurrentSockets` 512 (the ceiling; manifest `net.concurrentSockets` sets the number, the lower of the two applies), `defaultConcurrentSockets` 64, `concurrentFileHandles` 64, `inFlightOperations` 256. Past a socket or file-handle count, the call fails `limit` at once; past `inFlightOperations`, it waits in a queue of 256 for up to 10 s and then fails `limit` |
| Stream and datagram bounds (`LIMITS`, part 2) | ✅ | ✅ | ➖ | ➖ | `readWindowBytes` 1 MiB, `writeWindowBytes` 256 KiB, `inboundDatagramWindow` 256, `inboundDatagramWindowBytes` 1 MiB, `outboundDatagramWindow` 64, `maxDatagramBytes` 65507. Inbound datagrams past the window are dropped and counted |
| Web bounds (`LIMITS`, part 3) | ✅ | ✅ | ➖ | ➖ | `webContexts` 2, `webContextEvaluateMs` 60 000, `webContextIdleMs` 300 000, `webContextScriptBytes` 4 MiB, `webContextResultBytes` 1 MiB, `embedScriptBytes` 4 MiB |
| Provisional bounds (`LIMITS`, part 4) | ⚠️ | ⚠️ | ➖ | ➖ | `embeds` 32, `embedEventUrlBytes` 2 MiB and `secretBytes` 64 KiB carry an unconfirmed-number comment in [`limits.ts`](../../../src/contracts/limits.ts); the owner's confirmation settles each. | Control-channel pacing and call budgets | ✅ | ✅ | ✅ | ➖ | Per origin, control calls (`connect`, `connectSecure`, `listen`, `udpBind`, `lookup` and every path-based `fs` call, `readFileSync` included) share a bucket of 200 with a refill of 100 a second; past it the call fails `limit` at once. Handle I/O is paced at 4096 a second and waits up to 1 s. Page-side budgets: metadata 5 s, `fs` 15 s, `id` 5 s, `net` 35 s, grant 120 s, secrets 60 s, `web.evaluate` 65 s |
| `OrivonError` codes | ✅ | ✅ | ✅ | ⚠️ | 12 codes: `denied`, `revoked`, `unreachable`, `timeout`, `reset`, `closed`, `limit`, `invalid`, `notFound`, `exists`, `internal`, `unavailable`. | `hid` / USB | 🚫 | 🚫 | 🚫 | 🚫 | Cut from v0 for every tier ([`security-model.md`](../../architecture/security-model.md) §Capabilities excluded from v0). The permission gate denies `hid`, `serial` and `usb`, and no device is ever pre-granted |
| `subprocess` | 🚫 | 🚫 | 🚫 | 🚫 | Never a native process: one holds its user's whole authority, so no grant can bound it ([ADR-0040](../../decisions/ADR-0040-native-shaped-node-features-run-as-webassembly.md)). A child process is a WebAssembly program or an app module in a Worker of the app's own tab instead ([Table 3g](table-3g-running-code.md), `child_process`) |

**Only prefixes of ✅ are legal.** A call travels Spec'd, then Broker, then Page. Reading those three columns left to right, a cell may never be better than the one before it, with ⚠️ between ✅ and ❌. The valid shapes are therefore ❌❌❌, ✅❌❌, ✅✅❌ and ✅✅✅, and the ⚠️ variants of them. Anything else is a defect or a mislabel: Broker ✅ with Spec'd ❌ means the implementation ran ahead of the contract, which [ADR-0002](../../decisions/ADR-0002-capability-api-is-the-durable-asset.md) exists to prevent. `➖` and `🚫` cells are their own categories (no such layer, or excluded by design) and are not legality violations. The Node shim column is independent of the other three.

**What is proven end to end, and what is proven one layer short.** A real Electron launch proves a granted `net.connect` round-trips real bytes and a call outside the granted pattern is `denied` ([`e2e-capability-boundary.test.ts`](../../../test/capabilities/e2e-capability-boundary.test.ts)), and it drives `net.connectSecure`, `net.listen`, `net.udpBind`, the `fs` calls, `readFileSync`, `id`, `web.context` and `web.embed` through the real IPC pipe. Ordinary-build [`e2e-loopback-grant.test.ts`](../../../test/app-loading/e2e-loopback-grant.test.ts) proves an accepted consent dialog puts the manifest's grants on the origin (`app.grants()`) and that the served CSP enforces them. Five surfaces have no real-Electron test that asserts a call from a page's own script: `app.requestGrant`, both shapes of `fs.userSelected`, `secrets` and `net.lookup` (one network-dependent test, [`e2e-freetube-app.test.ts`](../../../test/ported-apps/e2e-freetube-app.test.ts), calls it and does not check the answer), and `fs.open`, which a real launch reaches only through a spawned component ([`e2e-child-process.test.ts`](../../../test/node-runtime/e2e-child-process.test.ts) and [`e2e-child-host.test.ts`](../../../test/node-runtime/e2e-child-host.test.ts)). Unit tests over the real `installOrivon` wiring and the control channel cover the other four. `secrets` has broker and Electron-shim unit tests only ([`secrets-capability.test.ts`](../../../src/broker/tests/secrets-capability.test.ts)): no test calls it through the preload and the control channel.

No automated test drives the whole chain "a real page, at a real public HTTPS origin, triggers the real install-time consent dialog and it works." The loader's install-origin check admits only public HTTPS origins, so no hermetic fixture can pass it, and every e2e test in this area substitutes one layer below that wall: a developer-only grant hook, a stubbed message box, or a direct call to the consent function itself ([`e2e-install-consent-journey.test.ts`](../../../test/app-loading/e2e-install-consent-journey.test.ts) runs the real `requestInstallConsent` against a real broker without launching Electron). Each link is proven for real (a real broker's consent decision, a real dialog's wiring, a real IPC round trip); the chain through a real public origin is not something CI can reach, which is a limit of CI. The mechanism has no gap there.

## Table 1b: authority no capability covers

This table lists 157 authorities a Node or Electron app can need, in 14 classes. It has one row for each authority that no member in Table 1a gives, and, so that no class has a hole in it, a short row for each authority Table 1a gives, which names the Table 1a row that gives it. A cell reads ❌ when nothing exists for it in that layer, and `➖ ➖ ➖ ❌` when the Node shim alone could answer with no contract change. A web-platform route has `➖` in the first three cells and a Note that starts "Web platform:", followed by the permission gate's answer. `🚫` replaces `➖` when an ADR refuses the route by design. The Node shim cell is `➖` too, unless a Node or Electron module covers the same authority (for example `http`, `clipboard`, `shell.openExternal`), in which case that cell says how the module fares. `HTTP(S) requests` and `WebSocket client` also have `✅` in the Broker and Page cells, because the page's own APIs are routed through `orivon.net`.

| A custom URL scheme answered from the app (`protocol.handle`, `registerSchemesAsPrivileged`) | ❌ | ❌ | ❌ | ❌ | [ADR-0047](../../decisions/ADR-0047-an-app-shows-pages-it-serves-itself-and-hears-a-shown-page-s-popups-and-downloads.md) serves a shown page from the app's own listener, not a scheme. An address on the app's own scheme inside a shown page does not load: the element's `will-navigate` names it, a subresource on it gets nothing, and it is never offered to another program. `protocol` refuses `unimplemented`. Layer: contract plus shell |
| `webContents` (zoom factor, navigation history, `printToPDF`, `findInPage`) | ❌ | ❌ | ❌ | ❌ | Refuses `unimplemented`; the shell owns zoom and history for a tab. Layer: shell |

### Lifetime

| Capability | Spec'd | Broker | Page | Node shim | Note |
|---|:--:|:--:|:--:|:--:|---|
| Running while the tab is hidden | ➖ | ➖ | ➖ | ➖ | Web platform: default Chromium background throttling applies to a tab; only the child host and the web-context host switch it off. Timers and seeding loops slow down. Layer: shell |
| Running after the tab is closed | ❌ | ❌ | ❌ | ❌ | Sockets, listeners and file handles close with the last document of the origin, and a child lives until the app's last page closes. | Running after the browser is closed | ❌ | ❌ | ❌ | ❌ | `window-all-closed` quits (except on macOS outside a private session), and every host and child ends. Daemons need more. Layer: shell |
| Running at system start, scheduled wake-ups, push messages | ❌ | ❌ | ❌ | ❌ | No autostart, scheduler or push service exists; service workers are the page's own. Mail and chat apps need them. Layer: shell |
| Background download and upload | ➖ | ➖ | ➖ | ➖ | Web platform: not measured; the shell's downloads list (Table 8) holds a tab's file downloads |
| What ends an app's children | ➖ | ➖ | ➖ | ✅ | Given: the app's last page closing, a reload of its only page, or `before-quit` ([ADR-0046](../../decisions/ADR-0046-an-app-s-children-live-until-its-last-page-closes.md)) |
